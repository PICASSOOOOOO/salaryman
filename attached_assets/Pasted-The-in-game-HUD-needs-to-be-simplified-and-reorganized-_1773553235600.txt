The in-game HUD needs to be simplified and reorganized. It should be smaller and positioned in the bottom-left corner of the screen. The chat interface should move to the bottom center, and the news ticker should be removed entirely. Instead, a small notification bar at the top of the screen should display only the most important news, and it should be limited to events within the player’s proximity. The current HUD is overwhelming: text is too small, too much information appears at once, and overall visibility is poor. The background lighting is also too dark, making it difficult to see the environment. The game needs proper daylight cycles, visible road textures like concrete, grass in empty lots, and clear environmental lighting. Street lamps should illuminate darker areas at night, but they should not flicker constantly—only during periods of social unrest or instability.

The city itself should be redesigned to be smaller and more consolidated. Right now it feels too spread out and underpopulated. Most of the population should be concentrated in the city, while the wastelands remain sparse and dangerous with only occasional clusters of activity. To reduce data usage and improve performance, there should be only one of each major institution: a bank, a general store, a public office or co-working institution similar to WeWork (with its own in-game brand), a hotel or hostel, a power plant, Pablo (the main tech store), and a newly branded weapon store. The city should also contain several apartment buildings, while suburbs exist on the outskirts. Empty lots should be available for purchase.

All NPCs—important or not—should follow the survival system. If the store is not operating, NPCs should hunt or scavenge for food. If they have no money, they should seek work. Store NPCs also need to be easier to approach. Buildings should have doors, visible interiors, and accessible interaction zones where players can see NPCs inside. Entering these spaces should trigger a zoomed-in interior scene where interaction happens. The interaction key (“E”) currently does not function well and needs refinement.

Important NPCs—especially those with jobs—should have deep storylines, robust dialogue systems, and more natural voice performances. Replicants should sound human, just like regular NPCs, while only androids should sound robotic so players can distinguish them. Replicants and rogue NPCs should generally be on the run, and there should be a Blade Runner–style faction responsible for hunting them. Player-controlled replicants should be immune to being hunted but still remain under suspicion.

Dialogue presentation also needs improvement. Text that appears above NPC heads disappears too quickly, is too long, and is difficult to read. Dialogue should appear more slowly and clearly. Random background voices should be minimized; most NPC chatter should appear as text, with voice acting reserved only for important characters during dedicated conversation scenes with zoomed-in camera angles and character portraits.

The mobile version currently contains far too much information and may not be practical to maintain as a fully playable version. The interface should instead be optimized for smaller screens and slower computers by making it narrower and simpler. Much of the clutter in the in-game HUD should be moved into the Terminal Hub, which acts as the main work interface. The in-game menu should be renamed “Terminal,” and most management features should live there rather than inside the gameplay HUD. The Terminal Hub should also contain global chat, private messaging, and direct access to Pablo AI.

The news system also needs to slow down. The ticker currently explains everything happening in the world too quickly. News should spread locally through NPC gossip within earshot of the player rather than globally. This will make the world feel more organic. Random voices should be reduced and replaced with slower text-based storytelling.

Pablo AI should be front and center in the Terminal Hub since it is the free forever feature. All other features should be separated and locked behind their respective seven-day free trials based on the pricing tiers already established.

The economy and player statistics also need adjustments. Net worth should represent starting cash plus the value of owned assets, not salary. A Gold Standard toggle should allow players to see how much their currency equals in gold, while a USD conversion should appear only inside the Terminal Hub main window. Gold and fiat balances should remain visible in the player HUD during gameplay. For health, the game should experiment with a heart-based system similar to Zelda rather than numerical HP values. Player stats should also include defense, physical attack, survival defense, and overall durability.

Faction roles and salaries should be clearly defined. “Corporate Suits” should simply be called “Suits,” and they should have the highest salaries at 500 fiat per minute. Replicants should earn 350 fiat per minute, and Outlaws should earn 200 fiat per minute. Starting net worth should be reduced to 25,000 for Suits, 18,000 for Replicants, and 10,000 for Outlaws. Suits control the military and police forces and primarily operate inside the city. Replicants and Outlaws interact more heavily with the wastelands.

Gang territories should be fully populated by their respective factions, and they should regularly raid rival factions. The wastelands should feel dangerous and active, especially for anyone other than Outlaws. A crafting system is also necessary so Outlaws can produce their own food and energy. Power infrastructure is critical to the success of Minx City, which makes it a major strategic target for both Replicants and Outlaws, while Suits must defend it.

Although the city has open borders, officials should still stop Outlaws and wasteland residents for identification checks. Entering the city is not illegal, but authorities are actively searching for criminals, gang members, drug dealers, and weapons traffickers. Merchants and traders should appear throughout both the city and the wastelands.

Finally, the game needs a comprehensive item system. Weapons should include melee and ranged options such as knives, dirks, short swords, long swords, foils, sabres, spears, axes, maces, rifles, shotguns, machine guns, laser weapons, plasma weapons, miniguns, pistols, revolvers of various calibers (.32, .44, .357), .22 rifles, sniper rifles, and improvised melee weapons like boxing gloves. Consumable items should include purified water, mineral water, energy packs, ballistic and energy ammunition, fruits and vegetables, and various meats including beef, chicken, pork, and more exotic or illicit items. Technological items should include radio implants, radar implants that expand player visibility, and high-end devices such as the Pablo Terminal MX-75, the Minx Terminal 50-50 personal computer, and the Public Terminal SYS-44 units placed around the city. The public terminals cannot be purchased and are reserved for enterprise-level players operating businesses in the world.
