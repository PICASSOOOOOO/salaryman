Keep the split pricing model, but require players to pay upfront at the beginning of their session. If they don’t have enough funds to start, they must go to the Admin building to register, so the Admin building needs fully developed menus to support this process. Reduce the number of buildings overall and keep only the essential ones, all located within a single central intersection: Admin, Pablo (tech store), Shadow Tower (Picasso HQ, partner hub, and primary location for much of the fictional story interior gameplay), Swordies (weapons), Go See Cal (vehicle dealer—this should be outdoors with multiple drivable vehicles that can be stolen or purchased), Landman (real estate office for rentals and purchases), and Banco Ombra (the bank, which must include both a functional ATM and full banking system so players don’t have to carry large amounts of money and risk theft; all players can open an account for 50,000 fiat). Buildings should visually match their actual number of floors, and when a player moves behind one, the camera should render it transparent so the player remains visible.

All bill management should be handled through the in-game terminal. Some bills are recurring, and if a player earns more than the equivalent of 25 gold daily (2.5 million fiat per day, or roughly two real-life hours), they can use in-game currency to pay for real-life subscriptions. Bills must be included in all rental and purchase agreements but charged separately—electricity, gas, internet, terminal fees (phone lines), security (within city limits), and property taxes every in-game month (28 days). Each bill should range from 25,000 to 100,000 fiat, making property ownership or renting feel expensive and pushing players to generate income. If players fail to pay bills, rent, or mortgages, they enter negative fiat and owe the bank. After three in-game months of debt, their assets are automatically liquidated, and the city repurchases their property at fair market value. During that debt period, other players can buy foreclosed properties by covering the outstanding debt and assuming ownership responsibilities.

Add a modern credit score system to both the main terminal and the in-game player menu, alongside survival stats like thirst, hunger, and energy. Introduce a wanted system tied to debt and crime, where police or bounty hunters actively pursue players. Redesign the map to include varied terrain such as mountains, hills, lakes, ponds, rivers, deserts, and dynamic weather like snow and rain. The wanted system should function as a debt collection mechanic: collectors will approach players and demand payment for outstanding crimes or debts. If the player cannot or refuses to pay, they can attempt to walk away, but this triggers combat—designed to be one of the most intense and difficult experiences in the game. Debt is tracked in fiat and can be repaid at the Admin building, which serves as a safe zone, along with smaller admin outposts. Finally, reduce unnecessary buildings and ensure NPCs remain inside, actively working or serving customers—no NPCs should appear idling on rooftops or in non-functional spaces.
