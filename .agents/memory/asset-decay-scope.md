---
name: Ecosystem condition, survival, and maintenance
description: Differentiated survival and degradation rules for people, replicants, pets, vehicles, buildings, and infrastructure.
---

The ecosystem uses a shared authoritative condition/maintenance framework with
different profiles by entity and asset type:

- Human players and NPCs rely on net worth and stamina for survival; secondary
  effects include stress, overwork, physical injury from fighting, bleeding, and
  sickness.
- Replicants rely on net worth and battery/stamina, and can suffer mechanical or
  computer failures such as malware and viruses.
- Pets include fish, cats, dogs, parrots and other birds, doves, snakes, iguanas,
  horses, cows, water buffalo, sharks, dolphins, whales, and elephants. They need
  food and water, with species-appropriate care, but routine care must be
  forgiving and infrequent enough not to become constant player micromanagement.
- Vehicles used outdoors—including bicycles, Vespas, cars, tanks, utility
  vehicles, and jeeps—degrade through issues such as rust, engine trouble, flat
  tires, and fuel needs.
- Buildings, floors, and systems also degrade: electrical, gas, and water
  infrastructure lose condition; bathrooms accumulate grime/graffiti; lights
  burn out; and fixtures need repair.

All physical assets and simulated living entities lose quality over time and
need maintenance. Keep the visual world’s gradual solarpunk-to-cyberpunk shift
slow; do not turn every condition into the same meter or give pets an excessive
care cadence.

**Why:** the user defined a survival office sim RPG MMO in which living beings,
vehicles, buildings, and infrastructure all lose condition and require
maintenance, with distinct needs for humans, replicants, and pets.

**How to apply:** build reusable, server-authoritative condition rules with
profile-specific needs and repair/care paths. Make pet feeding and watering
necessary but low-frequency. Let vehicles degrade during outside-world use.
Apply maintenance to buildings, floors, utilities, bathrooms, lights, and other
fixtures. Use elapsed-time degradation with gradual quality changes; never
degrade cash, account identity, or story progress.

left without upkeep. Use it as a reference for environmental neglect, not as
character approval or footage to reuse; the intended game view is top-down.

**Why:** the user clarified the intended slow solarpunk-to-cyberpunk progression
and that the trailer depicts environmental neglect over time.

**How to apply:** keep Tower footage in the Godot 3D top-down view; the trailer's
environmental wear does not approve a character design or authorize reusing its
