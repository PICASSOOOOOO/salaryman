---

Treat the Tower as a character-like entity, not only as a backdrop: it needs electrical power, plumbing, and ongoing upkeep to survive. Building electrical power is separate from replicant power and player stamina.

The default downloadable game is an MMO: all normal sessions connect to the same server, and building condition is shared across players. Custom servers built through GitHub are not hosted unless requested.

Pablo Corp is responsible for Tower power and water bills. Building service sales
and company-owned property sales or leases fund those costs; unpurchased office
inventory belongs to Pablo Corp, while resale and sublease proceeds after a
player purchase belong to that owner. Low operating funds raise Tower prices,
and unpaid bills can disable power and water. Emergency backup serves paid
clients and wealthy players first, then shared building infrastructure. Apply
these economic rules to the current single-building maze, not the old city-wide
simulation. Property settlement remains server-authoritative and supports
ATM-transferred income, FIAT, and GOLD.

**Why:** The user described the building as a living part of the game that players keep alive together, and clarified the shared-server model.

**How to apply:** Keep shared utility/upkeep state server-owned and visible across normal sessions; keep custom-server hosting opt-in. Do not conflate building electricity with character abilities.

**Why:** The user clarified that Pablo Corp owns unpurchased Tower inventory and pays the building's utility costs, and that backup eligibility prioritizes paid clients and wealthy players before infrastructure.

**How to apply:** Credit company-owned sales and leases to Tower operations, preserve private resale proceeds, and keep the cashflow, price pressure, outages, and backup policy local to this one building.

Pablo Corp's power and water bills must be paid at economically modeled rates
from Tower sales. Early-game prices and sales volume must keep ordinary
operations able to cover those bills; utility disasters should arise only much
later if sustained sales fail to meet costs.

Pablo Corp owns the initial shops, stores, and services the user has named so
far. Survival-related goods and services require payment from both players and
NPCs; their purchases are sales for the current business owner. This initial
ownership is not a permanent monopoly: outside businesses may enter, and
disruptors may dynamically change competitive conditions.

NPCs should develop AI-driven creative and innovative ambitions. They may leave
a job, join another employer, or start a business when they have enough capital.
Players and NPCs with sufficient funds may purchase a business. Business concepts
must remain unique by default; opening duplicate locations as a chain or franchise
requires the owner, player or NPC, to have net worth above ƒ1,000,000,000.

Pablo Corp pays partner shop owners; the shop owners hire and pay their own
employees. Treat shop owners as partners who can expand and build creative
businesses over time in the live MMO, rather than as employees on Pablo Corp's
direct payroll. Pablo Corp's profit must account for its payments to partners
and operating costs. If Tower infrastructure fails and sponsored shops close,
Pablo Corp suffers from lost business. Price all items and services with costs
and profit in mind; the user's target is 300% pricing, with markup versus margin
to be confirmed before encoding the formula.

The live MMO should grow at a deliberate pace: each city/server has a
five-year plan measured in real time.

**Why:** the user clarified that Pablo Corp pays partner shop owners, shop
owners pay employees, and the ecosystem should support gradual creative growth
over a five-real-time-year city/server plan.

**How to apply:** model partner payments separately from owner-managed payroll,
make shop-owner expansion and creative activity viable, and account for
infrastructure-driven shop closures as lost business. Pace the city/server
economy across five real-world years rather than compressing it into a short
in-game cycle.

**Why:** The user wants a creativity- and innovation-friendly economy where NPCs
can pursue new work or start businesses with capital. Business entry is open to
buyers who can afford it, while uniqueness and a net-worth threshold regulate
chains and franchises.

**How to apply:** Credit paid sales to the business's current owner. Allow outside
entry and dynamic competitive changes rather than hardcoding a permanent Pablo
monopoly. Let NPC ambitions affect career choices and entrepreneurship, but
require capital to start a business. Enforce unique businesses by default and
allow chains or franchises only above the ƒ1,000,000,000 net-worth threshold for
either players or NPCs. Balance early sales against recurring power and water
costs; allow price pressure or outages only after a sustained, market-driven
shortfall, not simply because the server economy is new.
