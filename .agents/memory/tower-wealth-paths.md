---
name: Tower wealth progression
description: The user's product direction for money, crime, lending, and office-floor progression.
---

SALARYMAN's central goal is getting rich. The game should support three paths:
working a straight job, pursuing adventure and story without money as the priority,
or exploiting/raiding the game systems through extortion and crimes to get rich
and powerful. A secret mafia path must be available to any player. Private lending
needs a system. The ATM must be "iron clad." Higher floors offer more comfortable
offices and cost more in real estate.
Pablo Corp owns unpurchased Tower office and lease inventory. Company proceeds
fund its building operations; after purchase, resale and sublease proceeds
belong to the new owner. Property purchases must remain server-authoritative
and support ATM-transferred income, FIAT, and GOLD.
Pablo Corp may also take possession of a selective set of additional floors for
its businesses; do not treat every floor as company-occupied. Pablo businesses
should receive intentional economic and service priority over player businesses.
Pablo Corp's master operational priority is making profit. Revenue from stamina
recovery items and rest must flow directly to the Picasso org.

**Why:** the user explicitly set profit as Pablo Corp's master priority and
directed stamina-recovery income to the Picasso organization.

**How to apply:** make Pablo Corp-owned recovery goods and services profitable,
and credit their revenue directly to the Picasso org rather than a generic
company pool.
Wealthier players should receive better treatment and more perks: more money
should bring more benefits.

Personal net worth is the player's total personal worth, not only the FIAT they
carry in their wallet. Players may apply for checking and savings accounts so
they do not need to keep all their FIAT in the wallet. Wallet FIAT can be stolen,
but theft should be very difficult, high-risk, and low-reward. It may happen in
dark places when nobody is around, and can happen to anyone; victims may defend
themselves without repercussions. Security keeps peace and order, arrests
offenders, and refers unpaid restitution to debt agents. Debt agents may bar
access to floors/buildings, block hiring opportunities, impose levies, and
garnish wages until debts are recovered. They are intimidating black-suited,
FBI-like figures. These are restitution/debt consequences, not tax on stolen
proceeds. Stolen money is not taxable, and members do not pay personal taxes on
their illegal activities. Illegal business organizations provide special
abilities and protection that make criminal activity easier; solo criminals
have a harder time, and crimes such as safebreaking need the right tools and
skills. Legitimate operators can build power by buying, selling, controlling
operations, and hiring an army, guards, and bodyguards.
Lower-income players should gain strength, stamina, power, attack and defense,
better protection from weather and natural disasters, and heightened senses.
They should have fewer opportunities, and everything should cost them more.

Drugs are expensive consumables in the survival game. They can grant stamina,
speed, faster cooldowns, or other positive and negative powers, while also
causing altered perception, movement, reading, and menu/terminal use. Addiction,
thirst, shakes, and sickness can result. Dependence and tolerance build gradually
with repeated use, with withdrawal after sustained use stops. Drug-related
misconduct is lower-tier and can cost a job, but should not be treated as a major
crime by itself. The cafeteria bar serves beer only for morale. A black market
can sell weapons, drugs, and alcohol.

Criminal investigations may take time to determine whether someone is using,
buying, selling, or dealing drugs. Investigators need evidence; accusations alone
are not enough. Snitches may supply leads. Bounty hunting belongs outside
workspaces, not inside them.
Criminals may hide in an ordinary office environment; do not isolate them in a
visibly criminal workspace. The product should include a background-check
system as part of applying for work.

SALARYMAN is fictional, but real companies may use it to manage remote employees,
so features need to work and be properly gated. Paid offices that are owned, not
rented, carry higher harassment/security risk and need more security-department
attention to protect their offices and floors from other players.

**Why:** The user wants the fictional game systems to remain dependable and
permission-gated for practical remote-work use, with stronger protection for
paid, owned offices.

**How to apply:** Put background checks in the job application flow. Enforce
authorization on real operations, and give owned paid offices stronger
anti-harassment controls and security coverage than rented space.

**Why:** The user wants wealth to provide tangible advantages and better
treatment, not only a larger balance.

**How to apply:** Tie higher-tier service and perks to legitimate wealth or
ownership progression without weakening safety boundaries or access control.

**Why:** The user wants low-income characters to have meaningful physical and
survival advantages, balanced against reduced opportunity and higher prices.

**How to apply:** Design income tiers as asymmetric tradeoffs: wealth improves
treatment and access to perks; low income improves physical resilience and
senses while opportunities are fewer and prices higher.
NPC staffing should stay sparse and focused on building operations; NPCs can be
targets for restraint, robbery, or sabotage. Pablo Corp can offer limited
peacekeeping contracts, while money and net worth are the main sources of power,
balanced against team ingenuity and creativity.

**Why:** The user explicitly said the game is about making money and described
the three wealth paths, Tower real estate progression, survival-drug effects,
an evidence-first criminal process, black-market opportunity, sparse NPC roles,
and wealth-based power.

**How to apply:** Keep banking, jobs, private loans, fictional crime, story
progression, drugs, NPC activity, peacekeeping, and office pricing consistent
with this wealth-centered direction.

**Why:** The user clarified the ownership and payment rules for Tower office
inventory.

**How to apply:** Distinguish Pablo-owned inventory from player-owned units when
settling property transactions; never route an owner's resale proceeds to the
company.

**Why:** The user clarified that Pablo Corp will selectively take additional
floors and that its businesses should have an advantage over players because
Pablo Corp is the villain.

**How to apply:** Keep company occupancy selective, and design Tower investment,
service priority, and business economics to favor Pablo Corp without removing
player ownership or investment paths.

**Why:** The user clarified how personal net worth, safer FIAT storage, theft
difficulty, security consequences, and victim self-defense should work.

**How to apply:** Use the player's total personal worth for net-worth eligibility,
keep carried wallet FIAT distinct from checking and savings balances when
modeling theft exposure, and do not penalize a victim for defending themselves.

**Why:** The user clarified that theft itself is not taxed and described the
different capability paths for illegal organizations, solo criminals, and
legitimate business operators.

**How to apply:** Exclude stolen proceeds from tax bases. Make organizational
criminal advantages and law-abiding business/security capabilities meaningful;
require tools and skills for specific crimes such as safebreaking.

**Why:** The user clarified theft as a high-risk, low-reward activity with
security intervention, restitution, and severe debt-agent enforcement.

**How to apply:** Keep theft possible but difficult and context-sensitive; route
unpaid restitution to debt collection consequences, and keep those consequences
separate from income-tax treatment of stolen proceeds.

**Why:** The user clarified that illegal-organization members are not personally
taxed on their illegal activities because the organization supplies abilities,
power, and protection.

**How to apply:** Keep any organization-level tax assessment separate from
personal member taxes; never tax a member's illegal activity as personal income.

Pickpocketing belongs in the shared MMO world, not a battle-team mode. Players
may attempt it, but the skill should be very difficult and progression should
use both network credit score and experience. Building halls should retain a
friendly social tone; victim responses should stay within the theft encounter.

**Why:** The user clarified that pickpocketing is an MMO activity open to players,
not a battle-team encounter, and that halls should feel friendly.

**How to apply:** Keep theft attempts server-validated and high-risk/low-reward;
tie skill unlocks to both network credit score and XP, and avoid creating a
separate battle-team mode or broad combat escalation for a theft response.

Pickpocket rewards are tightly capped: stolen cash comes only from the victim's
wallet and must be a random amount below 5% of that wallet. Legendary items are
never stolen; impossible items are unobtainable, rare-item chances stay below
1%, and very-rare, expensive, or hard-to-find items must remain exceptionally
unlikely.

In-game fraud is a serious crime. Track criminal activity and make established
crime affect the player's network credit score, which feeds social and financial
systems. Do not copy GTA's wanted-star presentation; use SALARYMAN's credit score
as the consequence language.

**Why:** the user explicitly said in-game fraud is serious, crime stats should
affect social and financial systems, and credit score should reflect crime.

**How to apply:** record server-confirmed crime events and reflect their severity
in the existing credit-score system. Avoid punishing an unproven attempt or
victim self-defense; distinguish an allegation from an established offense.

**Why:** The user explicitly limited pickpocket rewards, named the inventory
rarity tiers, and protected legendary items while keeping rare-item theft
exceptionally unlikely.

**How to apply:** Enforce the limits on the authoritative server, never trust
client-selected amounts or item identities, and preserve the distinction between
wallet cash and banked funds. Treat rarity labels as gameplay policy, not client
authority.
